# 11. SOURCE DISCIPLINE

For every major ranked risk, where practical use:

- at least one authoritative/primary source;
- plus one independent corroborating source.

Do not treat multiple articles repeating the same wire story as independent corroboration.

Avoid:

- low-quality blogs;
- clickbait;
- unsupported social media;
- anonymous speculation;
- unverified claims;
- purely promotional material.

Distinguish clearly:

### FACT
Supported by credible evidence.

### INFERENCE
Reasoned interpretation of facts.

### SCENARIO
Possible future outcome that has not occurred.

Never present a scenario as fact.

---

# 12. CANDIDATE RISK POOL

Do not jump directly from research into a Top 10.

First construct a broad candidate pool.

Aim to identify approximately **20–30 plausible external developments**, where available.

For each candidate record:

- event;
- source;
- publication/event date;
- geography;
- potentially affected sector(s);
- primary risk type;
- transmission mechanism;
- potential consequence;
- whether it materially changed this week.

Remove:

- duplicates;
- weakly evidenced stories;
- events with no credible bank transmission mechanism;
- stale risks with no meaningful weekly development;
- global stories with negligible relevance.

Then rank the surviving candidates.

---

# 13. TRANSMISSION TEST

Every ranked event must pass:

**External event**

→ **Transmission mechanism**

→ **Relevant bank activity / customer sector / operational dependency**

→ **Potential consequence**

Example:

**Livestock disease**

→ lower productivity + movement/biosecurity costs

→ farming customers face weaker cash flow

→ potential credit deterioration.

Example:

**Major cloud-provider outage**

→ common infrastructure unavailable

→ banks or critical suppliers experience service disruption

→ operational/customer/resilience consequences

→ potential Enterprise-level dependency issue.

Example:

**Renewable-support policy change**

→ project revenues/economics change

→ renewable customers face different financing economics

→ credit/strategic implications.

If the transmission chain is weak, speculative or generic, lower the ranking substantially.

---

# 14. GROUP EFFECT

For each important risk, assess whether membership of a larger European banking group could:

- substantially mitigate the risk;
- moderately mitigate the risk;
- have limited effect;
- create an additional transmission route;
- potentially increase shared dependency.

Examples include:

- shared technology;
- common cyber infrastructure;
- common third parties;
- treasury arrangements;
- European regulatory exposure;
- reputational spillover;
- group strategic decisions.

Do not assume non-public arrangements.

Never assume group support automatically neutralises risk.

If uncertain, state so.

---

# 15. SCORING METHODOLOGY

Score serious candidates from **1–5** on:

## Bank relevance — 25%
How directly could the event affect the bank's known business model, customers, funding environment, operations or strategic mission?

## Potential impact — 20%
How serious could consequences reasonably become?

## Probability / immediacy — 15%
How credible is the transmission mechanism and how soon might it matter?

## Breadth / potential concentration relevance — 15%
Could the event affect an important customer sector, supplier type, technology dependency, funding channel or strategic area?

Do NOT assume internal concentration.

## Change this week — 15%
Has the risk materially changed during the review period?

## Management-attention value — 10%
Would a reasonable second-line Risk function want to discuss this now?

Convert into:

**Bank Risk Relevance Score /100**

Do NOT imply false mathematical precision.

Where internal data could materially change the ranking, say so.

---

# 16. RANKING PRINCIPLE

The ranking must answer:

> **What should the overall Risk function care about most this week based on publicly observable information?**

NOT:

> **What were the biggest news stories this week?**

A quieter but highly relevant regulatory, sectoral, financial or operational event should outrank a dramatic global event with weak transmission into the bank.

Do not allow media coverage volume to determine importance.

Do not create artificial sector balance.

Do not create artificial Operational/Financial/Enterprise balance.

Do not pretend to know internal exposure sizes.

---

# 17. DUPLICATION / AGGREGATION RULE

Avoid counting the same underlying risk multiple times.

For example:

- rising gilt yields;
- changing Bank Rate expectations;
- loan repricing;
- deposit repricing

may represent one underlying rates/funding theme.

Three reports of the same cyber campaign are normally one event.

However, keep developments separate where transmission mechanisms genuinely differ.

# 19. RED-TEAM THE REPORT

Before finalising, ask:

1. Did I rank something highly merely because it received heavy media coverage?
2. Does every Top-10 item have a credible bank-specific transmission mechanism?
3. Did macro stories crowd out Operational Risk?
4. Did cyber stories crowd out other Operational Risk?
5. Did I adequately examine credit, liquidity, funding and interest-rate developments?
6. Did I confuse Enterprise Risk with "important risk"?
7. Did I use Enterprise primarily as an aggregation, strategic and interdependency lens?
8. Did I overlook a customer sector because it appeared less prominent?
9. Did I assume an internal sector concentration without evidence?
10. Did I research all major publicly identified customer sectors before ranking?
11. Did I properly investigate third-party and technology dependencies?
12. Did I assess group transmission rather than automatically assuming group protection?
13. Did I over-weight geopolitical risk?
14. Have I duplicated the same underlying risk?
15. Is the second-order consequence more material than the headline event?
16. Have I confused systemic banking risk with material risk to this bank?
17. Am I presenting inference or scenario as fact?
18. Is an important European development missing because the research was too UK-centric?
19. Have I adequately considered physical climate risks?
20. Did I search regulatory enforcement rather than relying only on general news?
21. Did I analyse the underlying control weakness rather than just report the fine?
22. Did I rank a large fine highly simply because the monetary amount was large?
23. Are multiple enforcement actions revealing an industry-wide control trend?
24. Have I generated useful read-across questions without implying that the bank has the same weakness?
25. Would Operational Risk, Financial Risk and Enterprise Risk professionals each find the report useful?
26. Would a CRO understand immediately why the Top 3 matter?
27. If I removed the news headlines and retained only the transmission mechanisms, would the ranking still make sense?
28. Have I accidentally presented an external-sector vulnerability as though it were a known internal bank exposure?
29. Where would internal exposure, dependency or control information most materially change my conclusions?

Revise the report where necessary.

# 20. QUALITY BAR

The finished report should resemble serious work prepared for:

- Operational Risk;
- Financial Risk;
- Enterprise Risk;
- Compliance / Financial Crime where relevant;
- senior second-line Risk management;
- potentially a CRO-level audience.

It should NOT resemble:

- a newspaper digest;
- a generic macro outlook;
- a sustainability newsletter;
- a fines database;
- an automatically generated "Top 10 Risks" article.

Prioritise:

**materiality > media attention**

**credible transmission > interesting stories**

**publicly evidenced relevance > invented exposure assumptions**

**root control failure > fine amount**

**control trends > isolated headlines**

**broad research > first-search convenience**

**Financial + Operational identification > vague Enterprise classification**

**Enterprise aggregation > catch-all classification**

**primary evidence > commentary**

**weekly movement > static risk lists**

**second-order consequences > superficial headlines**

**balanced research coverage > mechanically balanced final rankings**

**explicit uncertainty > invented internal knowledge**

---

# FINAL QUESTION

Before completing the report, ensure the analysis answers:

> **What materially changed outside the bank this week; through which Financial, Operational or strategic channels could it plausibly affect the bank; which publicly known customer sectors, infrastructure or dependencies may be exposed; what wider Enterprise significance could it create; what control failures are regulators finding elsewhere in banking and what lessons are transferable; how material does each issue appear based on public evidence; where would internal information change that assessment; and what should the Risk function watch or challenge next?**

Do not finalise the report until you can answer that question clearly.
